The imported rows cannot be edited here. The file in data/ is the authority for those and this table is a copy of it, so an edit made here would be undone by the next import. To change one, replace the CSV and re-run python import_hospital_data.py.

Nothing here is added by hand. The archive mirrors the hospital’s file, and a row typed in on this screen would be a fact with no source behind it. A patient the hospital has never seen is recorded on their own chart instead, where it is attached to a person rather than floating in the dataset.

One line per person. Somebody who attended more than once has each visit listed in the History column rather than repeating their name down the table.

Name and date of birth identify the person; the patient number is what the practice links to. Two people can share a name, so the number is the join and the name is how you recognise it.

Clinical trial patient is empty for most rows, and that is normal — the archive covers everyone the hospital has ever seen, and only a handful of them are registered with this practice for screening. A name appears once somebody enters that patient number on their chart.

It means registered for screening, not found eligible. Whether somebody actually qualifies is decided on the Trials screen, against a protocol.
